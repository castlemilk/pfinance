## Design Context

### Users

PFinance serves Australian individuals, couples, households, families, roommates, and financial caregivers. They use the product to understand spending, coordinate shared money, manage budgets, and identify risks without needing financial expertise. The primary product job is to turn transaction data into a useful decision quickly. The north star is time to first useful insight, with a target of under two minutes for a new user and under thirty seconds on returning analytics visits.

### Brand Personality

Warm, trustworthy, and tactile. PFinance should make sensitive financial information feel calm, legible, and approachable while still conveying precision and security. The voice is plain-spoken and practical. It explains what changed, why it matters, and what the user can do next.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the established refined retro-futurist and skeuomorphic language: warm CRT-inspired palettes, restrained phosphor accents, inset data surfaces, raised controls, and physical depth. Preserve the four selectable palettes and both light and dark modes. Use readable sans-serif typography for prose and headings, with monospace tabular numerals for financial data.

Avoid flat Material or Vercel-style minimalism, cold neon cyberpunk, generic glassmorphism, noisy 2010-era skeuomorphism, and decorative effects that compete with financial meaning. Depth should communicate hierarchy or interactivity. Glow, scanlines, and motion should be subtle and palette-aware.

Accessibility targets WCAG 2.1 AA. All core workflows must work with keyboard and screen readers. Charts need equivalent textual summaries, colour cannot be the only differentiator, controls need at least a 40px target, and motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Design Principles

1. **Lead with the decision**: show the most useful conclusion before exposing chart controls or analytical technique.
2. **Make scope unmistakable**: personal and group data must be visibly labelled and technically isolated at every layer.
3. **Treat money as auditable data**: prefer integer cents, consistent periods, explicit assumptions, and locale-aware formatting.
4. **Use tactility with restraint**: reserve depth, glow, and motion for hierarchy, feedback, and state changes.
5. **Design every state**: loading, empty, partial-data, error, locked, and populated states must all explain the next step.

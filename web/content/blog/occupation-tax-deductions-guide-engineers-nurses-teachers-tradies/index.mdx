Whether a purchase is tax-deductible in Australia depends directly on **how you earn your income**. A pair of non-slip steel-capped boots is a standard `D3` protective clothing deduction for an electrician on a construction site, but is completely non-deductible for a software engineer working in a home study.

Because PFinance's [Tax Deduction Tracker](/features/tax-deductions/) uses your occupation context when reviewing expenses against ATO categories `D1` through `D15`, understanding the rules for your specific profession helps you capture every legitimate claim—and avoid the claims the ATO routinely flags. Whatever you claim, the substantiation rules in [how to keep track of expenses for taxes](/blog/keep-track-of-expenses-for-taxes-australia/) apply to every occupation.

<Callout type="note" title="Record-keeping vs tax advice" corner="ATO substantiation">
This guide summarizes common occupation-specific deduction categories and record-keeping rules published by the ATO. It is general information, not personal tax advice.
</Callout>

## 1. Software Engineers, Designers & IT Professionals

For technology and knowledge workers, the largest legitimate deductions almost always sit in **Working From Home (`D5`)**, **Depreciating Home-Office Equipment (`D5`)**, and **Self-Education (`D4`)**:

- **Working from home running expenses (`D5`)**: Compare the 67c/hr Fixed Rate Method against the Actual Cost Method using our [WFH Deduction Calculator](/tools/wfh-deduction-calculator/).
- **Computer peripherals, monitors, and ergonomic chairs (`D5`)**: Items costing **$300 or less** (such as a mechanical keyboard, webcam, or cables) can be claimed immediately for their work-use percentage; items over $300 (laptops, monitors, standing desks) are depreciated over their effective life.
- **Technical subscriptions & cloud sandboxes (`D5`)**: Work-related developer tools, professional memberships (e.g. ACS/IEEE), and technical books not reimbursed by your employer.
- **Commonly rejected**: Normal home-to-office commuting (even if you answer Slack messages on the train), conventional office clothing, and coffee/lunch bought while working from home.

## 2. Nurses, Midwives, Doctors & Allied Health Workers

Healthcare workers incur recurring registration, uniform, and continuous professional development costs every year:

- **Annual registration & union fees (`D5`)**: AHPRA annual registration renewal, nursing/medical union fees (e.g. ANMF, AMA), and professional indemnity insurance.
- **Compulsory uniforms, scrubs & non-slip footwear (`D3`)**: Branded hospital uniforms, occupation-specific scrubs, non-slip nursing shoes (where required for safety beyond conventional footwear), and laundry claims ($1 per load for work-only loads, or 50c per load if mixed, up to $150 without written laundry receipts).
- **Equipment & CPD (`D4`/`D5`)**: Stethoscopes, fob watches, penlights, and compulsory continuing professional development (CPD) seminars.
- **Commonly rejected**: Plain black trousers or normal sneakers that could be worn outside work, and travel from home to your regular hospital shift.

## 3. Teachers & Education Professionals

Teachers frequently spend their own money on classroom preparation and spend evenings/weekends marking and planning from home:

- **Working from home prep & marking (`D5`)**: Hours spent lesson-planning, writing reports, and marking at home qualify for WFH running expenses—make sure you keep a contemporaneous hours diary across all four school terms!
- **Teacher registration & union fees (`D5`)**: Annual state teaching registration (VIT, NESA, QCT, TRBWA, etc.) and union membership fees.
- **Outdoor playground & sport duty sun protection (`D3`)**: Sunglasses, sunhats, and SPF sunscreen proportionate to outdoor supervision duties.
- **Excursions, camps & inter-campus travel (`D1`/`D2`)**: Travel between two school campuses or transporting students/equipment to sporting venues.

## 4. Tradies, Technicians & Construction Workers

Employed tradies often carry the highest dollar value of work-related tool and vehicle deductions—which also means the ATO scrutinizes tradie receipts and logbooks closely:

- **Tools and trade equipment (`D5`)**: Power tools, hand tools, toolboxes, and test meters. Individual tools costing **$300 or less** (and not part of a set costing over $300) can be written off immediately; tools over $300 are depreciated over their effective life.
- **Protective clothing & outdoor protection (`D3`)**: Steel-capped boots, high-vis shirts, hard hats, safety glasses, heavy-duty drill cotton workwear, laundry, and outdoor sunscreen/sunglasses.
- **Work-related vehicle expenses (`D1`/`D6`)**: Normal home-to-work travel is private—**except** in limited circumstances where you must transport bulky tools or equipment (such as an extension ladder or heavy compressor) essential for your work AND your employer provides **no secure storage** on site. Always keep a valid 12-week logbook and fuel/service receipts.

Salary sacrificing into superannuation (or packaging a car on a novated lease) is one of the most popular ways for Australian employees to reduce their income tax. By directing pre-tax dollars into super up to the annual concessional contributions cap, those dollars are generally taxed at 15% inside the super fund instead of your marginal tax rate plus Medicare levy.

However, thousands of Australians get an unexpected bill at tax time because they assume reducing their **Taxable Income** also reduces their **HELP/HECS repayment** or keeps them below the **Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)** threshold. It does not.

Here is how the math actually works, and how to model it before you change your payroll settings.

## How Pre-Tax Super Salary Sacrifice Reduces Income Tax

When your employer pays your salary, they must pay the compulsory **Superannuation Guarantee (SG)** on top of your ordinary time earnings. Both your employer's SG contributions and any voluntary pre-tax salary sacrifice (or personal deductible super contributions) count toward your **annual concessional contributions cap**.

### Example: $110,000 Base Salary

Suppose you earn $110,000 and decide to salary sacrifice **$10,000** into super over the financial year:

1. Your **Taxable Income** drops from $110,000 to **$100,000**.
2. Instead of paying your marginal income tax rate plus the 2% Medicare levy on that $10,000, your super fund deducts **15% contributions tax** ($1,500), putting **$8,500 net** into your retirement balance.
3. Your immediate annual tax saving is the difference between your marginal tax rate (plus Medicare levy) and the 15% super tax rate.

You can test the exact impact on your weekly, fortnightly, or monthly take-home pay in our [Australian Salary Calculator](/tools/australian-salary-calculator/), and project how that extra $8,500/year compounds over 20 years in the [Super Calculator & Estimator](/tools/super-calculator/).

## The Repayment Income Trap: Why Salary Sacrifice Does Not Lower HECS/HELP

If you have a **HELP, HECS, VSL, or STSL student loan**, the ATO does **not** calculate your compulsory repayment using your Taxable Income. Instead, it uses **Repayment Income (RI)**:

> **Repayment Income** = Taxable Income + Reportable Super Contributions + Reportable Fringe Benefits + Total Net Investment Loss + Exempt Foreign Employment Income

What does this mean in practice?

- When you salary sacrifice $10,000 into super, your Taxable Income drops by $10,000—**but** your employer reports that $10,000 as a **Reportable Employer Superannuation Contribution (RESC)** on your income statement.
- The ATO adds that $10,000 right back in when calculating your HELP repayment!
- Even worse: if your payroll software only withholds PAYG tax based on your reduced $100,000 cash salary, **too little HELP repayment may be withheld during the year**, leaving you with a tax bill when you lodge your return.

## The Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS) and Reportable Fringe Benefits

The same "add-back" rule applies to the **Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)**: the extra 1% to 1.5% surcharge levied on higher earners who do not hold an appropriate level of private patient hospital cover for the full financial year.

Income for MLS purposes adds back:
- Reportable super contributions (salary sacrifice and personal deductible super)
- Reportable fringe benefits (such as charity/NFP salary packaging or a taxable novated lease)
- Net investment losses

As a result, you **cannot** use super salary sacrifice to drop below the Medicare Levy Surcharge threshold. In fact, if you package a benefit that creates a grossed-up Reportable Fringe Benefit Amount (RFBA)—including certain FBT-exempt electric vehicle novated leases, which are still reportable on your income statement!—your Income for MLS Purposes and Repayment Income can actually **increase**.

## Checklist Before Setting Up Salary Sacrifice

1. **Check your remaining concessional super cap**: Ensure your employer SG plus your proposed salary sacrifice will not exceed the annual concessional cap (unless you are eligible to use carry-forward unused concessional cap amounts from the prior 5 years and your Total Super Balance was under $500,000 at 30 June).
2. **Model your net pay and HELP repayment**: Enter your salary, student loan toggle, and voluntary super amount in the [Australian Salary Calculator](/tools/australian-salary-calculator/), and check the compulsory repayment under the new marginal schedule in the [HECS-HELP Calculator](/tools/hecs-help-calculator/) (explained in our [2025–26 repayment changes guide](/blog/hecs-help-repayment-changes-2025-26-marginal-rates-indexation/)).
3. **Project your long-term retirement balance**: See what an extra $100 or $200 a fortnight does to your future balance in the [Super Calculator](/tools/super-calculator/).
4. **Track your work-related tax deductions**: Unlike salary sacrifice, legitimate work-related deductions (D1–D15) reduce **both** your Taxable Income and your Repayment Income. Keep your receipts organized all year with PFinance's [Tax Deduction Tracker](/features/tax-deductions/).

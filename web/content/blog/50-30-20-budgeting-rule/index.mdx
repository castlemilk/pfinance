Managing your money doesn't have to be complicated. The 50/30/20 rule is one of the most straightforward budgeting frameworks that can help you take control of your finances without feeling overwhelmed.

## What is the 50/30/20 Rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting guideline that divides your after-tax income into three categories:

- **50% for Needs** — Essential expenses you can't avoid
- **30% for Wants** — Discretionary spending for enjoyment
- **20% for Savings & Debt** — Building your financial future

This framework was popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren in her book "All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan."

## Breaking Down Each Category

### 50% — Needs

Needs are expenses that are essential for your basic living and working requirements. These include:

- **Housing**: Rent or mortgage payments, council rates, strata levies, basic home insurance
- **Utilities**: Electricity, water, gas, basic NBN internet
- **Transportation**: Car repayment, compulsory third-party (CTP) and rego, fuel, public transport (Opal/Myki/Go Card)
- **Groceries**: Supermarket essentials (not dining out or Uber Eats)
- **Health**: Essential medical costs, prescriptions, and private hospital cover
- **Minimum debt payments**: Required monthly payments on loans and credit cards

</Callout>

### 30% — Wants

Wants are non-essential expenses that enhance your quality of life but aren't strictly necessary:

- **Dining out and entertainment**: Restaurants, pubs, movies, concerts
- **Subscriptions**: Streaming services, gym memberships, apps
- **Shopping**: Clothing beyond basics, gadgets, home decor
- **Hobbies**: Sports equipment, craft supplies, gaming
- **Travel**: Holidays and weekend getaways
- **Upgraded services**: High-tier NBN or mobile plans with extra data

The key is being honest with yourself. A basic mobile plan is a need; the latest flagship smartphone on a high-cost plan is a want.

### 20% — Savings and Debt Repayment

This category focuses on building financial security beyond your compulsory employer Superannuation Guarantee:

- **Emergency fund**: Aim for 3-6 months of essential expenses in a high-interest savings or mortgage offset account
- **Voluntary superannuation**: Concessional (salary sacrifice) contributions or First Home Super Saver (FHSS) contributions, which you can project with our [Super Calculator](/tools/super-calculator/)
- **Extra debt payments**: Paying more than the minimum on credit cards, car loans, or your mortgage
- **Investments**: Broad-market ASX and international index ETFs
- **Short-term savings goals**: Home deposit, car replacement fund, or holiday sinking fund

<Callout type="warning">
Minimum debt payments go under "Needs." Extra payments beyond the minimum count toward this 20% category.
</Callout>

## How to Apply the 50/30/20 Rule

### Step 1: Calculate Your After-Tax Income

Start with your take-home pay—the amount deposited into your bank account after income tax, the Medicare levy, and any compulsory HELP/HECS repayments. You can model your exact weekly, fortnightly, or monthly net pay using our [Australian Salary Calculator](/tools/australian-salary-calculator/).

**Example**: If your gross salary is $90,000/year (plus super), your after-tax take-home income is roughly $5,600/month.

### Step 2: Apply the Percentages

Using a $4,000/month take-home example (or enter your own pay in the [Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator/)):
- **Needs (50%)**: $2,000
- **Wants (30%)**: $1,200
- **Savings (20%)**: $800

### Step 4: Make Adjustments

If your spending doesn't align with the guideline:

1. **Too much on needs?** Look for ways to reduce fixed costs
2. **Too much on wants?** Identify subscriptions or habits you can cut back on
3. **Not enough savings?** Automate transfers to savings accounts

## When to Modify the 50/30/20 Rule

This framework is flexible. Here are some situations where you might adjust the percentages:

### High Cost of Living Areas

If you live in an expensive city, your needs might take up 60% of your income. Adjust to 60/20/20 temporarily while working toward higher income or lower costs.

### Aggressive Debt Payoff

If you're tackling high-interest debt, consider a 50/20/30 split with that extra 10% going toward debt repayment.

### Building Wealth Faster

If your needs are covered and you want to accelerate savings, try 50/20/30 with 30% going to investments.

### Low Income Situations

When money is tight, survival takes priority. Focus on covering needs first, then savings, with whatever remains for wants.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Miscategorizing wants as needs**: That fancy coffee subscription? That's a want.

2. **Not including irregular expenses**: Annual insurance payments, car maintenance, and holiday gifts should be factored in.

3. **Ignoring lifestyle inflation**: As income grows, resist the urge to increase spending proportionally.

4. **Being too rigid**: Life happens. Some months will vary, and that's okay.
